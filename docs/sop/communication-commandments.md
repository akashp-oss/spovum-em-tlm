# Bang Design — Communication Commandments & SOP

THE COMMUNICATION COMMANDMENTS  

Draft Standard Operating Procedure for Customer-Facing Emails

Purpose  

Eliminate radio silence. Keep the customer informed early so we never waste time building the wrong thing. Reduce friction. No customer email goes out that the rest of the team cannot stand behind.

The Spine  

Every customer email, in this order:  

1. What I have done (or what I am about to do)  

2. Why I did it (the reason or logic)  

3. How it benefits the customer (the clear value or next step)

---

Commandment 1 — Never go dark  

Send an update the same day you start work or receive a new request. Silence creates doubt and misalignment. A short “I have started / I have received” note is better than waiting for perfection.

File follow-up (mandatory)  

If you send files (drawings, reports, photos, packs, quotes), sending them is not the last step. By the next morning, send a short follow-up and ask:  

- whether they were able to download and view the files, and  

- whether they need any assistance (access, format, missing pages, a different file type).  

Do this even if they have not replied. Do not wait for them to report a problem. If they confirm receipt, log it and stop chasing. If there is no reply by the next morning after the follow-up, send one more note the same day, then escalate internally.

Commandment 2 — Follow the Spine in every email  

Structure every customer email around What / Why / Benefit, in that order.

Commandment 3 — Speak like a professional adult  

Use clear, precise, formal language. No slang, no casual abbreviations, no emoji-heavy tone. Write as if the email will be forwarded to the customer’s leadership.

Commandment 4 — Zero tolerance for typos and errors  

Proofread every email before sending. Read it aloud. Check names, dates, numbers, and attachments. A single careless error damages credibility.

Commandment 5 — Flag schedule impact immediately  

If a new request affects critical milestones (prototype fabrication, tooling, release dates), state it clearly and early:  

“Accommodating this request will require X additional hours and will push the current timeline by Y days. The impact will fall on [specific milestone].”

Commandment 6 — Always quantify the effort  

Tell the customer how many hours the request will take. Example: “This change is estimated at 6–8 hours of work.” Vague statements create friction; numbers create clarity.

Commandment 7 — Prefer short, frequent updates over long, delayed ones  

Do not wait until the work feels “ready.” Send progress notes while work is still in motion. This keeps the customer’s expectations aligned with reality.

Commandment 8 — End every email with a clear next step or question  

Never leave the customer wondering what happens next. Close with one specific action or decision needed from them.

Commandment 9 — Assume a new reader  

Assume the email will be read by someone who was not in the previous conversation. Include just enough context so a new reader can understand the situation without hunting through old threads.

Commandment 10 — When in doubt, send the update  

If you are hesitating because the work is incomplete or imperfect, that is exactly when the customer needs to hear from you. Silence is the greater risk.

Commandment 11 — Coordinate internally before you send  

Do not write to the customer until the people who will actually do the work have seen the message and agreed with it.

Never  

- Promise a delivery date, a holiday slip, or a new owner without speaking to the person covering the work.  

- Assume a stand-in is available because they were named last week.  

- Send from sick leave or travel and leave the rest of the team to discover the promise in the customer’s inbox.

Always  

1. Name the person who will execute the next step.  

2. Confirm they are in office on the days you are about to promise.  

3. Agree the date, the hours, and the files with them before the customer email goes out.  

4. Put them on copy (or put your manager on copy if they are unavailable).  

5. If you cannot reach a covering person, do not invent a date. Write to your manager first. Only then write to the customer.

If two people are out (you ill, the stand-in on leave), that is an internal problem. It must be solved internally before the customer is told a date.

Internal check (before every customer email)  

If any answer is no, the email stays internal.

- Have I named who will do the next piece of work?  

- Have I spoken to that person today (message or call), not assumed?  

- Have they confirmed they are available on the date I am about to promise?  

- Have they confirmed the hours / files / milestone in the draft?  

- Are they on copy, or is my manager on copy if they are not reachable?  

- If I am ill or out, has the covering person read this exact draft?

Correct sequence  

1. An ill colleague tells the covering person and manager.  

2. The cover person confirms a real date.  

3. Only then write to the customer, with the covering person on copy.

Templates

A) Standard customer email (the Spine)

Subject: [Clear, specific subject]

Dear [Name],

What I have done / am doing:  

[1–2 sentences]

Why:  

[1 sentence]

How this benefits you:  

[1 sentence]

Schedule / effort (if relevant):  

This request is estimated at X hours. Accommodating it will impact [milestone] by approximately Y days.

Next step:  

Please confirm [specific decision] by [date] so we can proceed.

Best regards,  

[Your name]

B) File follow-up (next morning)

Subject: Follow-up — files sent [date] — [project / PO / topic]

Dear [Name],

What I have done:  

Yesterday I sent [file names / pack description] for [project].

Why:  

I am confirming that the files reached you in a usable form, so work does not stall on a download or format issue.

How this benefits you:  

If anything did not open, we can resend or change the format today and keep [milestone] on track.

Next step:  

Please confirm that you were able to download and view the files. If you need any assistance, tell me what you saw (error, missing attachment, wrong format) and I will resolve it.

Best regards,  

[Your name]

C) Internal handover BEFORE the customer email

To: [Covering person]  

Cc: [Manager]  

Subject: Handover before customer update — [project / PO]

I am unwell / out and need to update the customer.

What I have done: [status]  

What I proposed to tell them: [draft date / message]  

What I need from you: Confirm you can deliver by [date], or give me a date you can stand behind. I will not send the customer email until you reply.

If you are also out, please say so. I will escalate rather than guess.

D) Line to add after a confirmed handover

Who is handling this while I am out: [Name], copied here. They have confirmed delivery on [date].

Do not name a colleague in a customer email unless that colleague has agreed to be named.

---

Daily habit  

Before you finish any customer-related task / before you send:  

1. Have I told the customer what I just did, why, and how it helps them?  

2. Did I send files today? If yes, put a next-morning follow-up on the calendar.  

3. Internal gate: the person who must deliver this has confirmed the date.
